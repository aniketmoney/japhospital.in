Total Knee Replacement

When Knee Pain Starts Limiting Everyday Life

Total knee replacement replaces damaged joint surfaces with implant components. It may be considered for severe arthritis or joint damage when pain, stiffness and loss of function continue despite appropriate non-surgical treatment.

Who may need an evaluation?

  • Persistent knee pain during walking, stairs or daily activities
  • Marked stiffness or reduced movement
  • Pain at rest or at night
  • Progressive deformity or difficulty standing and walking
  • Symptoms that remain disabling despite non-surgical care

Before surgery is recommended

  • Clinical examination and imaging
  • Review of medicines, physiotherapy and other non-surgical options
  • Discussion of medical conditions and surgical fitness
  • Shared decision on implant and surgical approach
  • Recovery and home-support planning
1–3 days*Common hospital-stay range
Early walking*Usually with walker/crutches
PhysiotherapyImportant from early recovery
Several months*Full recovery varies

Robotic vs conventional replacement

Conventional and robotic-assisted knee replacement are both surgeon-led procedures. Robotic-assisted systems can add 3D planning and real-time navigation to help the surgeon execute the plan. Suitability depends on the patient and the surgeon's assessment.

Implant options

Implant design and selection depend on anatomy, diagnosis, bone quality, activity needs and the surgeon's preferred system. The consultation should explain why a particular implant is being recommended.

Frequently asked questions

How soon can I walk after knee replacement?

Patients are commonly encouraged to begin short-distance walking early after surgery with assistance or a walking aid. The exact timing and weight-bearing plan are set by the surgical and physiotherapy team.

How long does recovery take?

Recovery is gradual. Many daily activities improve over weeks, while strength, confidence and the full benefit of surgery can continue improving over several months.

When should I consult Dr. Aman Singh?

Consider an orthopaedic consultation when knee pain, stiffness, swelling or reduced mobility is repeatedly interfering with walking, stairs, sleep or normal daily activities.

*These are general patient-education ranges, not a promise of JAP Hospital's individual protocol. Dr. Aman Singh should review and approve all medical timelines before the website is published.
ROSA® Robotic Knee Replacement

Technology-Assisted Planning, Surgeon-Controlled Execution

Robotic-assisted joint replacement combines detailed planning with intra-operative navigation. The technology supports the surgeon; it does not replace the surgeon's judgement or decision-making.

What the technology can support

  • Pre-operative or intra-operative planning
  • Alignment and positioning guidance
  • Real-time navigation during surgery
  • Personalised execution based on the surgical plan

Questions to ask at consultation

  • Am I suitable for robotic-assisted replacement?
  • What changes compared with conventional surgery?
  • Which implant system is planned?
  • What recovery plan will I follow?
Spine Surgery

Assessment for Disc, Nerve Compression & Degenerative Spine Conditions

Spine treatment depends heavily on the diagnosis. Many patients do not need surgery; surgical treatment is considered only after examination, imaging and a review of appropriate non-surgical options or when urgent neurological concerns are present.

Reasons to seek specialist review

  • Persistent back or neck pain with leg/arm symptoms
  • Numbness, tingling or weakness
  • Walking limitation related to nerve compression
  • Symptoms that are worsening or not improving with treatment

Consultation may include

  • Neurological and musculoskeletal examination
  • Review of X-ray/MRI or other imaging
  • Discussion of physiotherapy, medicines or injections when appropriate
  • Explanation of minimally invasive or other surgical options when indicated
Hip Replacement

Restoring Mobility When Hip Joint Damage Becomes Severe

Hip replacement may be considered for advanced arthritis or joint damage causing significant pain and loss of function. Evaluation includes symptoms, examination, imaging, medical fitness and discussion of expected recovery.

Arthroscopy

Keyhole Joint Evaluation & Treatment

Arthroscopy uses a small camera and instruments through small incisions for selected joint problems. The exact procedure and recovery depend on the joint and condition being treated.

Fracture & Trauma Care

Prompt Orthopaedic Assessment for Injuries

Fractures, dislocations and serious musculoskeletal injuries need timely assessment. Treatment may range from splints and casts to reduction, fixation or surgery depending on the injury.

Other Speciality Guides

Multispeciality Consultation & Support

The following sections provide general patient information. The exact evaluation, investigations and treatment plan depend on the individual patient and the consulting specialist.

General Medicine

Adult Medical Assessment & Ongoing Health Management

General medicine supports adults with new symptoms, ongoing medical conditions and medical optimisation before or after surgery. Consultation is based on history, examination and investigations when clinically required.

Common reasons to consult

  • Fever, weakness, fatigue or unexplained symptoms
  • Blood-pressure, diabetes or other chronic-condition review
  • Respiratory, digestive or general medical concerns
  • Medical fitness and optimisation before planned surgery

Evaluation may include

  • Detailed medical history and physical examination
  • Review of current medicines and previous reports
  • Blood tests, imaging or other investigations when indicated
  • Referral or coordination with another specialist when needed
General Surgery (Laparoscopic)

General Surgical Evaluation With Minimally Invasive Options

General surgery covers a range of conditions that may require operative treatment. Where suitable, laparoscopic techniques use small incisions and a camera-assisted approach; the final method is selected after specialist assessment.

Consultation may be useful for

  • Abdominal or groin swelling and suspected hernia
  • Gallbladder or selected abdominal surgical concerns
  • Persistent symptoms for which surgery has been advised
  • Review of an existing surgical diagnosis or report

Planning usually considers

  • Clinical examination and review of imaging/reports
  • Whether surgery is necessary and which approach is appropriate
  • Anaesthesia and medical-fitness assessment
  • Expected hospital stay, wound care and follow-up
Urology

Assessment for Urinary, Kidney, Bladder & Prostate Concerns

Urology focuses on the urinary system and related male reproductive concerns. Symptoms are assessed with history and examination, followed by urine tests, blood tests, ultrasound or other investigations when appropriate.

Reasons to seek a urology review

  • Pain or difficulty while passing urine
  • Frequent urination, poor urinary flow or retention symptoms
  • Suspected kidney or urinary stones
  • Blood in urine or recurrent urinary concerns

Possible next steps

  • Urine and blood investigations
  • Ultrasound or other imaging when indicated
  • Medication or procedure-based treatment depending on diagnosis
  • Follow-up planning and prevention advice where relevant
Critical Care

Close Monitoring for Patients Requiring Higher-Acuity Support

Critical-care support is intended for patients who require closer observation and coordinated medical management because of serious illness, unstable vital signs, emergency needs or post-operative concerns.

Care may involve

  • Frequent monitoring of vital signs and clinical status
  • Emergency stabilisation and rapid medical assessment
  • Post-operative observation when a patient needs closer supervision
  • Coordination between the treating surgeon, physician and care team

Important note

The level of monitoring and treatment required is determined by the treating clinicians. In an emergency, patients should seek immediate medical attention rather than relying on website information.
